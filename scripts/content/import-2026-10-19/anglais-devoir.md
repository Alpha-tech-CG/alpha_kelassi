TEXT: Eating too much Salt
Also known as chloride, salt is added to food to make it tastier. Salt provides your body with sodium, which is necessary for proper muscle function regulating the amount of water in the body. The average daily sodium intake for most Americans should be 1500 milligrams, while the maximum intake is 2300 milligrams, consuming more sodium than the maximum daily recommended intake is harmful.
Hypertension or high blood pressure is a condition that affects nearly 50 million Americans. While this condition can be caused by other factors such as stress and eating diets rich in saturated fats, excessive intake of sodium increases the risk of developing it. If you already suffer from high blood pressure, reducing your salt intake could also reduce your risk of heart disease and stroke, according to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention.
Also known as oedema, fluid retention occurs when excess fluid accumulates in your body, including the skin. This condition can be caused by hormone changes, hot weather and high salt intake. It is characterized by swelling in the hands, ankles, and feet, and a feeling of stiffness or aching. Reducing your salt intake and drinking a lot of water can help to alleviate this condition.
Although a definite link between salt intake and cardiovascular disease is yet to be established, most studies agree that excessive consumption of salt increases the risk of cardiovascular disease. This is because ingesting sodium triggers a domino effect. It causes water retention, which can elevate blood pressure. Hypertension, in turn, can increase the risk of heart attack, stroke and the development of cardiovascular disease. So many physicians prefer not to take any chances. As researchers who penned "Sodium, blood pressure and cardiovascular disease" for the journal of the American Heart Association said: "Scientific evidence for the benefit of sodium reduction is strong, and sodium is often an unnecessary food additive in the era of refrigeration. The challenge for the healthcare community and our society is to effect a meaningful reduction in sodium intake."
Rebecca Howard; Updated November 21, 2018
