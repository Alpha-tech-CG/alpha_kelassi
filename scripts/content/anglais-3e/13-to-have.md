Le verbe **to have** (avoir) sert à exprimer la **possession**. Il a deux façons de se dire au présent : **have / has** et **have got / has got**. Les deux ont le même sens.

> **📘 À retenir**
> **to have** = *avoir* (possession). Formes : **have** (I, you, we, they) et **has** (he, she, it). On peut aussi dire **have got / has got**.

## 1. La forme affirmative

| Pronom | to have | to have got | Exemple |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | have | have got (I've got) | I **have** a bike. / I **have got** a bike. |
| You | have | have got (You've got) | You **have** a book. |
| He / She / It | has | has got (He's got) | She **has** a car. / She **has got** a car. |
| We | have | have got (We've got) | We **have** a dog. |
| They | have | have got (They've got) | They **have** two children. |

**Formes contractées :**
- I have → I've · You have → You've · We/They have → We've / They've
- He/She/It has → He's / She's / It's *(got)*

**Exemples :**
- My parents **have** a car. = My parents **have got** a car.
- Moloto **has** a red bike. = Moloto **has got** a red bike.

## 2. La forme négative

- Avec **have** : on utilise **do not have / does not have**.
- Avec **have got** : on ajoute **not** → **have not got / has not got**.

| Pronom | avec *have* | avec *have got* |
|---|---|---|
| I / you / we / they | do not have (don't have) | have not got (haven't got) |
| he / she / it | does not have (doesn't have) | has not got (hasn't got) |

**Exemples :**
- My parents **don't have** a car. = My parents **haven't got** a car.
- Moloto **doesn't have** a red bike. = Moloto **hasn't got** a red bike.

## 3. La forme interrogative

- Avec **have** : **Do / Does + sujet + have…?**
- Avec **have got** : **Have / Has + sujet + got…?**

**Exemples :**
- **Do** you **have** a pen? = **Have** you **got** a pen? *(As-tu un stylo ?)*
- **Does** she **have** a sister? = **Has** she **got** a sister?

**Réponses courtes :**
- Yes, I **do**. / No, I **don't**. — Yes, I **have**. / No, I **haven't**.
- Yes, she **does**. / No, she **doesn't**. — Yes, she **has**. / No, she **hasn't**.

## 4. Expressions idiomatiques avec *to have*

Ici **to have** ne veut pas dire « posséder » mais « prendre / faire » une action.

| Anglais | Français |
|---|---|
| to have breakfast / lunch / dinner | prendre le petit-déjeuner / déjeuner / dîner |
| to have a shower / a bath | prendre une douche / un bain |
| to have a rest / a nap | se reposer / faire une sieste |
| to have a look | jeter un coup d'œil |
| to have a good day | passer une bonne journée |

**Exemples :**
- I **have breakfast** at seven o'clock. *(Je prends le petit-déjeuner à sept heures.)*
- Have a **good day**! *(Passe une bonne journée !)*
