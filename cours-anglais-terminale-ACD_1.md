# PART 1 — THE SIX GENERAL OBJECTIVES / LES SIX OBJECTIFS GÉNÉRAUX

This short guide explains what each objective requires and how to work on it. The nine themes (Part 2) are the content through which these skills are practised.

### OG1 — Listening / Comprendre des messages oraux
**Goal:** understand spoken English (conversations, requests, announcements).
- Key content: socialising (greetings, introductions), making and answering **requests**, **direct and indirect speech**, **intonation**.
- *FR :* entraînez-vous à repérer le **ton** (question, ordre, surprise) et les **mots-clés**, sans chercher à tout comprendre.
- Strategy: listen for **gist** first (general idea), then for **details** (names, numbers, places).

### OG2 — Speaking / S'exprimer oralement
**Goal:** speak clearly and take part in a conversation or debate.
- Key content: **active/passive voice**, **reported speech**, taking turns, expressing and defending an opinion in a **debate**.
- *FR :* apprenez des **structures toutes faites** pour donner son avis (*In my opinion…, I agree/disagree because…*).
- Strategy: fluency before perfection; use **connectors** (*first, then, however, so*).

### OG3 — Reading / Comprendre des messages écrits
**Goal:** understand written texts (articles, letters, reports).
- Key content: **skimming** (idée générale), **scanning** (information précise), **prefixes/suffixes**, **cohesion** (linking words).
- Strategy: read the title and first sentences first; **guess unknown words from context** before using a dictionary.

### OG4 — Writing / S'exprimer par écrit
**Goal:** write correct, organised texts.
- Key content: **formal/informal letters**, **summary**, **report**, **argumentative essay**.
- Structure: introduction → development (paragraphs) → conclusion; one idea per paragraph.
- *FR :* soignez les **connecteurs logiques** et la **ponctuation**.

### OG5 — Learner Training / Prendre des initiatives dans l'apprentissage
**Goal:** become an autonomous learner.
- Key content: using a **dictionary** efficiently, **guessing from context**, **self-assessment** (checking one's own progress).
- *FR :* tenez un **carnet de vocabulaire** par thème et révisez régulièrement.

### OG6 — Reinforcing Skills / Développer les compétences acquises
**Goal:** consolidate and prepare for the BAC.
- Key content: **grammar revision**, **thematic vocabulary**, **BAC practice** (timed papers).
- Strategy: practise **past papers** under exam conditions and correct yourself with the keys.

## THEME 1 — FOOD AND HEALTH / ALIMENTATION ET SANTÉ

### Objectives
- Talk about food, diet and health; give advice; use modals of advice and quantifiers.

### Essential vocabulary (EN — FR)
- a balanced diet — une alimentation équilibrée
- healthy / unhealthy — sain / malsain
- to eat well — bien manger
- nutrients — les nutriments
- vitamins — les vitamines
- junk food — malbouffe
- to gain / lose weight — prendre / perdre du poids
- disease / illness — maladie
- to prevent — prévenir
- cure / treatment — remède / traitement
- hygiene — hygiène
- malnutrition — malnutrition
- to recover — guérir, se rétablir
- overweight / obesity — en surpoids / obésité
- to exercise — faire de l'exercice

### Reading text (pedagogical text, written for this course)
> **Eating Well to Live Well**
> Health begins on the plate. A balanced diet gives the body the energy and nutrients it needs to grow and to resist disease. In many African cities, people eat more and more sugary drinks and fried street food. These foods are cheap and tasty, but eaten every day they lead to weight problems and illness. Doctors advise us to eat fruits and vegetables, to drink enough water, and to exercise regularly. Prevention, they say, is always better than cure. A healthy life is not expensive: a plate of beans, greens and fruit often costs less than junk food, and protects the body far better.

### Comprehension questions
1. Where does health "begin", according to the text?
2. What kind of food is becoming common in African cities?
3. What problems can these foods cause?
4. Give three pieces of advice the doctors give.
5. Is a healthy diet necessarily expensive? Justify with the text.

### Answer key
1. Health begins "on the plate" (with what we eat).
2. Sugary drinks and fried street food (junk food).
3. Weight problems and illness.
4. Eat fruits and vegetables; drink enough water; exercise regularly.
5. No: the text says a plate of beans, greens and fruit often costs less than junk food and protects the body better.

### Grammar focus — Quantifiers & modals of advice
- **Countable / uncountable:** *many* apples / *much* water; *a few* / *a little*.
- **some / any:** *some* in affirmative, *any* in questions and negatives.
- **should / ought to / had better** for advice: *You should eat more fruit.*

### Practice exercises
A. Choose *much* or *many*: (1) How ___ water do you drink? (2) How ___ meals a day?
B. Complete with *should* or *shouldn't*: (1) You ___ eat junk food every day. (2) You ___ drink enough water.

### Exercise key
A. (1) much (2) many. — B. (1) shouldn't (2) should.

### Oral activity
**Debate:** *"Street food should be banned near schools."* Divide into two groups (for / against); each gives three arguments with examples.

### Writing task
Write a short paragraph (80–100 words): *"How to stay healthy as a student."*
**Model answer:**
> To stay healthy, a student should eat a balanced diet with fruit and vegetables instead of junk food. Drinking enough water and sleeping well are also important. Regular exercise, such as walking or football, keeps the body strong and the mind clear. Finally, good hygiene prevents many diseases. Prevention is better than cure: small daily habits protect our health far more than any medicine.

### Revision sheet — Food & Health
- Key idea: *a balanced diet + exercise + hygiene = health; prevention > cure.*
- Grammar: quantifiers (much/many, some/any), advice (should/ought to/had better).

## THEME 2 — DEVELOPMENT / LE DÉVELOPPEMENT

### Objectives
- Discuss development (economic, social, human); use comparatives and the present perfect.

### Essential vocabulary (EN — FR)
- development — le développement
- developing / developed country — pays en développement / développé
- growth — la croissance
- poverty — la pauvreté
- infrastructure — les infrastructures
- education — l'éducation
- standard of living — niveau de vie
- resources — les ressources
- investment — l'investissement
- sustainable development — développement durable
- literacy rate — taux d'alphabétisation
- progress — le progrès
- aid — l'aide
- debt — la dette
- to improve — améliorer

### Reading text (pedagogical text, written for this course)
> **What Does "Development" Really Mean?**
> For a long time, development was measured only by money: the richer a country, the "more developed" it was said to be. Today, people understand that development is also about **human life**: good schools, hospitals, clean water and roads. A country has truly developed when its citizens live longer, learn more and enjoy a better standard of living. Africa is rich in resources, yet many Africans remain poor. The challenge is to turn these resources into schools, jobs and infrastructure — and to do so in a **sustainable** way, so that future generations also benefit.

### Comprehension questions
1. How was development measured "for a long time"?
2. What else does development include today?
3. When has a country "truly developed"?
4. What paradox about Africa does the text mention?
5. What does "sustainable" development protect?

### Answer key
1. Only by money (the richer the country, the more developed).
2. Human life: schools, hospitals, clean water, roads.
3. When citizens live longer, learn more and have a better standard of living.
4. Africa is rich in resources, yet many Africans remain poor.
5. The interests of future generations.

### Grammar focus — Comparatives & present perfect
- **Comparatives/superlatives:** *richer than, more developed than, the richest.*
- **Present perfect** for results: *The country has built many schools.* (*FR :* lien passé-présent.)

### Practice exercises
A. Comparative: (1) A city is ___ (big) than a village. (2) This is ___ (good) solution.
B. Present perfect: (1) They ___ (improve) the roads. (2) Literacy ___ (increase) since 2000.

### Exercise key
A. (1) bigger (2) the best/a better. — B. (1) have improved (2) has increased.

### Oral activity
**Presentation:** Each student presents one factor of development (education, health, roads…) and explains why it matters for Congo.

### Writing task
Essay (120 words): *"Education is the key to development." Do you agree?*
**Model answer:**
> I strongly agree that education is the key to development. First, educated citizens can find better jobs and improve their standard of living. Secondly, education produces the doctors, engineers and teachers a country needs to build hospitals, roads and schools. Thirdly, it reduces poverty and ignorance, and helps people make informed choices. Of course, education alone is not enough: a country also needs investment and good governance. However, without education, no lasting development is possible. Therefore, investing in schools is investing in the future of the nation.

### Revision sheet — Development
- Key idea: *development = money + human life (health, education, infrastructure), sustainably.*
- Grammar: comparatives/superlatives; present perfect for results.

- Talk about leisure, talent and discipline; use the simple past and "used to".

### Essential vocabulary (EN — FR)
- a singer / musician — un chanteur / musicien

- rhythm / melody — rythme / mélodie

- talent — le talent

### Reading text (pedagogical text, written for this course)
> **More Than Entertainment**
> Music and sport are often seen as simple entertainment, but they are much more. Music carries the soul of a people: Congolese rumba, for example, is known across the world and tells the story of a nation. Sport, too, teaches discipline, team spirit and respect for rules. A footballer does not become a champion by luck: he trains for years, loses many matches and learns from defeat. For young people, music and sport can be a path out of poverty and a source of pride — but only with hard work, patience and discipline.

### Comprehension questions
1. How are music and sport "often seen"?
2. What does Congolese rumba tell, according to the text?
3. Name three things sport teaches.
4. How does a footballer become a champion?
5. What qualities does the text link to success?

### Answer key
1. As simple entertainment.
2. The story of a nation / the soul of a people.
3. Discipline, team spirit, respect for rules.
4. By training for years, losing matches and learning from defeat (not by luck).
5. Hard work, patience and discipline.

### Grammar focus — Simple past & "used to"
- **Simple past** for finished actions: *He won the match yesterday.*
- **used to** for past habits: *She used to sing in a choir.*

### Practice exercises
A. Simple past: (1) They ___ (win) the cup last year. (2) He ___ (not/train) enough.
B. "used to": Rewrite — *In the past, I played football every day.* → *I ___.*

### Exercise key
A. (1) won (2) did not train / didn't train. — B. I used to play football every day.

### Oral activity
**Dialogue:** two friends argue about who is the greatest — a famous musician or a famous athlete — each giving reasons.

### Writing task
Paragraph (80–100 words): *"My favourite sport or kind of music, and why."*
**Model answer:**
> My favourite sport is football because it brings people together. When our national team plays, the whole country watches and shares the same emotion. Football also teaches me discipline and team spirit: a single player cannot win alone. Every week I train with my friends, and even when we lose, we learn something. For me, football is not only a game — it is a school of life.

### Revision sheet — Music & Sports
- Key idea: *music and sport = culture + discipline + a possible path out of poverty.*
- Grammar: simple past; *used to* (past habits).

## THEME 4 — THE NORTH/SOUTH DIVIDE / LA FRACTURE NORD-SUD

### Objectives
- Discuss inequalities between rich and poor countries; use the passive voice.

### Essential vocabulary (EN — FR)
- the North / the South — le Nord / le Sud
- rich / poor countries — pays riches / pauvres
- inequality — l'inégalité
- gap / divide — le fossé / la fracture
- trade — le commerce
- raw materials — les matières premières
- exploitation — l'exploitation
- fair trade — commerce équitable
- debt — la dette
- aid / assistance — l'aide
- global / globalisation — mondial / mondialisation
- migration — la migration
- to benefit from — bénéficier de
- cooperation — la coopération
- dependence — la dépendance

### Reading text (pedagogical text, written for this course)
> **A Divided World**
> Our world is divided between a rich "North" and a poorer "South". The countries of the North consume most of the world's resources, while many countries of the South, although rich in raw materials, remain poor. Why? Part of the answer lies in **trade**: raw materials are often exported cheaply, while finished products are bought back at a high price. Debt and unfair trade keep the gap wide. Yet the South is not powerless. Through education, fair trade and regional cooperation, Southern countries can transform their resources themselves and reduce their dependence on the North.

### Comprehension questions
1. How is the world "divided", according to the text?
2. What paradox affects many Southern countries?
3. How does trade keep the gap wide?
4. Name two things besides trade that keep the gap wide.
5. What three solutions does the text suggest?

### Answer key
1. Between a rich North and a poorer South.
2. They are rich in raw materials but remain poor.
3. Raw materials are exported cheaply; finished products are bought back at a high price.
4. Debt and unfair trade.
5. Education, fair trade and regional cooperation.

### Grammar focus — The passive voice
- Formation: *be* + past participle. *Raw materials **are exported** cheaply.*
- Use: when the action matters more than who does it. (*FR :* voix passive = l'objet devient sujet.)

### Practice exercises
A. Put into the passive: (1) They export coffee. → Coffee ___. (2) The North consumes most resources. → Most resources ___.
B. Active or passive? *The problem was caused by debt.* → (passive/active?)

### Exercise key
A. (1) Coffee is exported. (2) Most resources are consumed by the North. — B. passive.

### Oral activity
**Debate:** *"Rich countries have a duty to help poor countries."* For / against, with arguments.

### Writing task
Essay (120 words): *"How can Southern countries reduce the North/South gap?"*
**Model answer:**
> Southern countries can reduce the gap in several ways. First, they should invest in education, because skilled citizens can transform raw materials into finished products instead of exporting them cheaply. Secondly, regional cooperation allows African countries to trade with one another and to build common infrastructure. Thirdly, fair trade ensures that producers receive a just price for their work. Finally, good governance is essential, so that resources benefit the whole population and not only a few. The gap will not close overnight, but with education, cooperation and justice, the South can take control of its own development.

### Revision sheet — North/South Divide
- Key idea: *unequal trade + debt widen the gap; education, fair trade, cooperation narrow it.*
- Grammar: passive voice (be + past participle).

- to grow up — grandir

### Reading text (pedagogical text, written for this course)
> **What Makes a Life "Famous"?**
> Famous lives are not always lives of wealth. Many of the people we admire most became famous because they **fought for others**. Nelson Mandela, who spent twenty-seven years in prison, dedicated his life to the fight against injustice and became a symbol of freedom. Great scientists, doctors, artists and sportspeople also leave a legacy that inspires new generations. Their stories teach us an important lesson: fame that lasts is not built on money alone, but on courage, hard work and service to others.

### Comprehension questions
1. Are famous lives "always lives of wealth"?
2. Why do we most admire many famous people, according to the text?
3. What is said about Nelson Mandela?
4. Name three other kinds of people who leave a legacy.
5. What lesson do their stories teach?

### Answer key
1. No, not always.
2. Because they fought for others.
3. He spent 27 years in prison and dedicated his life to the fight against injustice; he became a symbol of freedom.
4. Scientists, doctors, artists, sportspeople (any three).
5. Lasting fame is built on courage, hard work and service, not on money alone.

### Grammar focus — Past tenses & relative clauses
- **Past simple / past perfect:** *He had struggled for years before he succeeded.*
- **Relative pronouns:** *who* (people), *which/that* (things), *whose* (possession): *The leader **who** fought for freedom…*

### Practice exercises
A. Combine with a relative pronoun: *Mandela was a leader. He fought for freedom.* → *Mandela was a leader ___ fought for freedom.*
B. Past perfect: *Before he became president, he ___ (spend) years in prison.*

### Exercise key
A. …a leader **who** fought for freedom. — B. had spent.

### Oral activity
**Presentation:** present the life of a person you admire (African or foreign): birth, struggles, achievements, legacy.

### Writing task
Biography (120 words): *Write about a person you admire.*
**Model answer:**
> The person I admire most is Nelson Mandela. He was born in South Africa in 1918 and grew up under apartheid, a cruel system of racial injustice. As a young lawyer, he began to fight for the rights of black people. Because of this fight, he was arrested and spent twenty-seven years in prison. Yet he never gave up his ideals. When he was finally freed, he chose forgiveness instead of revenge and became the first black president of his country. Mandela's legacy is a lesson of courage, patience and reconciliation that still inspires the whole world.

### Revision sheet — Famous Lives
- Key idea: *lasting fame = courage + work + service to others.*
- Grammar: past simple/past perfect; relative clauses (who/which/that/whose).

### Reading text (pedagogical text, written for this course)
> **Moving People and Goods**
> Transport is the blood of a country's economy: it carries people to work and goods to market. In fast-growing African cities, however, roads are often crowded, and traffic jams waste hours every day. Too many old vehicles also cause pollution and accidents. Good transport means more than new cars; it means safe roads, reliable public buses, and perhaps one day modern railways. If cities invest in public transport, they will reduce traffic, pollution and accidents at the same time. Better transport will mean a better life for millions of people.

### Comprehension questions

2. What problem affects fast-growing African cities?
3. What do old vehicles cause?
4. What does "good transport" really mean, according to the text?

### Answer key
1. Because it carries people to work and goods to market.
2. Crowded roads and traffic jams that waste hours.
3. Pollution and accidents.
4. Safe roads, reliable public buses, and modern railways (not just new cars).
5. Traffic, pollution and accidents.

### Grammar focus — Future forms
- **will** for predictions: *Cities will reduce traffic.*
- **be going to** for plans/evidence: *They are going to build a new road.*
- **First conditional:** *If cities invest, they will reduce pollution.*

### Practice exercises

B. First conditional: *If the government ___ (build) railways, traffic ___ (decrease).*

### Exercise key
A. (1) is going to (2) will. — B. builds; will decrease.

### Oral activity
**Debate:** *"Public transport is better than private cars."* For / against.

### Writing task
Report (120 words): *Suggest three ways to improve transport in your town.*
**Model answer:**
> **Report: Improving Transport in Our Town.** Our town suffers from heavy traffic and frequent accidents. I would suggest three improvements. First, the local government should develop reliable public buses, so that fewer people use private cars. Secondly, the main roads should be repaired and widened to reduce traffic jams. Thirdly, stricter safety rules and controls on old vehicles would reduce both accidents and pollution. If these measures are taken, journeys will be faster and safer, and the air will be cleaner. Good transport is essential for the development of our town.

### Revision sheet — Transport
- Key idea: *transport = economy; invest in public transport → less traffic, pollution, accidents.*
- Grammar: *will* / *be going to*; first conditional.

## THEME 7 — DEMOGRAPHIC, ECOLOGICAL AND SOCIAL PROBLEMS / PROBLÈMES DÉMOGRAPHIQUES, ÉCOLOGIQUES ET SOCIAUX

### Objectives

- population growth — croissance démographique
- overpopulation — surpopulation
- birth rate — taux de natalité
- environment — l'environnement
- pollution — la pollution
- deforestation — la déforestation

- to pollute — polluer
- to protect — protéger

- consequence — la conséquence

### Reading text (pedagogical text, written for this course)
> **Living Together on One Planet**
> The world's population is growing quickly, especially in developing countries. More people need more food, more water, more houses and more jobs. When cities grow too fast, slums appear, and unemployment and poverty rise. At the same time, human activity harms the environment: forests are cut down, rivers are polluted, and the climate is changing. These problems are linked: a damaged environment makes poverty worse, and poverty pushes people to damage the environment. The solution is not to stop growing, but to grow **wisely** — protecting nature, educating citizens and sharing resources fairly.

### Comprehension questions
1. Where is the population growing quickly?
2. What appears when cities grow too fast?
3. Give two ways human activity harms the environment.
4. How are environmental and social problems "linked"?
5. What solution does the text propose?

### Answer key
1. Especially in developing countries.
2. Slums; unemployment and poverty rise.
3. Forests are cut down; rivers are polluted (climate changes).
4. A damaged environment worsens poverty, and poverty pushes people to damage the environment.
5. To grow wisely: protect nature, educate citizens, share resources fairly.

### Grammar focus — Cause & consequence
- **Cause:** *because, since, as, due to.*
- **Consequence:** *so, therefore, as a result, consequently.*
- *FR :* reliez toujours un problème à sa **cause** et à sa **conséquence**.

### Practice exercises
A. Link with *because* or *so*: (1) Cities grow fast, ___ slums appear. (2) Rivers are polluted ___ factories dump waste.
B. Choose the connector of consequence: *The forest was cut down; ___ the soil became poor.* (because / therefore)

### Exercise key
A. (1) so (2) because. — B. therefore.

### Oral activity
**Debate:** *"Economic growth always harms the environment."* For / against.

### Writing task
Essay (120 words): *"What can young people do to protect the environment?"*
**Model answer:**
> Young people have an important role in protecting the environment. First, they can change their daily habits: avoiding plastic, saving water and electricity, and planting trees. Secondly, they can raise awareness in their families and communities, explaining why pollution and deforestation are dangerous. Thirdly, they can join clubs and associations that clean streets and protect nature. Education is the key, because people protect what they understand. If every young person takes small actions, together we can reduce pollution and build a cleaner, healthier country for future generations.

### Revision sheet — Demographic/Ecological/Social Problems
- Key idea: *fast growth + damaged environment + poverty are linked; grow wisely.*
- Grammar: cause (*because/due to*) & consequence (*so/therefore*).

## THEME 8 — EMPLOYMENT / L'EMPLOI ET LE MONDE DU TRAVAIL

- Talk about jobs, unemployment and job-seeking; write a formal letter / apply for a job.

- unemployment — le chômage
- to apply for a job — postuler à un emploi

### Reading text (pedagogical text, written for this course)
> **Finding Work in a Changing World**
> For young Africans, finding a job is one of the greatest challenges. Every year, thousands of graduates leave school, but there are not enough jobs for everyone. Work is not only a way to earn a living; it is a source of dignity.

3. What is becoming "a real solution"?

2. Some wait for the government to hire them; others create their own work.
3. Self-employment (starting a small business, learning a trade).
4. Skills, experience and a good attitude.

## COMPLETE TERMINALE GRAMMAR / GRAMMAIRE COMPLÈTE DE TERMINALE

*A clear reference of the grammar required for the BAC. Each point gives the rule, the form and short examples.*

### 1. The tenses / Les temps

**1.1 Present simple** — habits, general truths.
- Form: base verb (+ *s* in the 3rd person singular). *She works. Water boils at 100°C.*
- Markers: *usually, every day, often, always.*

**1.2 Present continuous** — action happening now / temporary.
- Form: *am/is/are + V-ing.* *They are studying now.*
- Markers: *now, at the moment, look!, listen!*

**1.3 Past simple** — finished action in the past.
- Form: V-ed (regular) / 2nd column (irregular). *He travelled. She went.*
- Markers: *yesterday, last week, in 2010, ago.*

**1.4 Past continuous** — action in progress in the past.
- Form: *was/were + V-ing.* *I was reading when he called.*

**1.5 Present perfect** — past action linked to the present (result, experience).
- Form: *have/has + past participle.* *I have finished. She has never seen it.*
- Markers: *already, yet, just, ever, never, since, for.*
- *FR :* lien passé-présent ; **never** + date précise (on ne dit pas *I have seen him yesterday*).

**1.6 Past perfect** — an action before another past action.
- Form: *had + past participle.* *When I arrived, they had already left.*

**1.7 Future**
- *will* + base verb (prediction, decision now): *It will rain.*
- *be going to* (plan, evidence): *I am going to study medicine.*
- Present continuous (fixed arrangement): *I am meeting him tomorrow.*

### 2. The conditionals / Les conditionnelles
- **Zero** (general truth): *If + present, present.* — *If you heat ice, it melts.*
- **First** (real future): *If + present, will + base.* — *If he studies, he will pass.*
- **Second** (unreal present): *If + past, would + base.* — *If I were rich, I would travel.*
- **Third** (unreal past): *If + past perfect, would have + p.p.* — *If I had known, I would have come.*

### 3. The passive voice / La voix passive
- Form: *be* (correct tense) + past participle. *English is spoken here. The letter was sent.*
- Use: the action/object matters more than the doer; the doer is introduced by *by*.
- Active → Passive: *They build houses.* → *Houses are built (by them).*

### 4. Reported (indirect) speech / Le discours rapporté
- **Statements:** tenses shift back one step. *"I am tired," he said.* → *He said (that) he was tired.*
- **Questions:** use *if/whether* (yes-no) or the question word, normal word order. *"Where do you live?"* → *She asked where I lived.*
- **Orders:** *tell/ask + object + to + base.* *"Sit down,"* → *He told me to sit down.*
- Changes: *now→then, today→that day, here→there, this→that, tomorrow→the next day.*

### 5. Modals / Les modaux
- **Ability:** can / could. *She can swim.*
- **Permission:** can / may. *May I come in?*
- **Possibility:** may / might / could. *It might rain.*
- **Obligation:** must / have to. *You must stop.*
- **Prohibition:** mustn't. *You mustn't smoke.*
- **No obligation:** don't have to. *You don't have to come.*
- **Advice:** should / ought to / had better. *You should rest.*

### 6. Comparatives and superlatives / Comparatifs et superlatifs
- Short adjectives: *-er / -est.* *tall → taller → the tallest.*
- Long adjectives: *more / the most.* *interesting → more interesting → the most interesting.*
- Irregular: *good→better→best; bad→worse→worst; far→farther/further.*
- Equality: *as … as.* *He is as tall as his brother.*

### 7. Relative clauses / Les propositions relatives
- *who* (people), *which/that* (things), *whose* (possession), *where* (place), *when* (time).
- *The man **who** called… / The book **which** I read… / The town **where** I was born…*

### 8. Quantifiers / Les quantifieurs
- Countable: *many, a few, few, a number of.*
- Uncountable: *much, a little, little, a great deal of.*
- Both: *some, any, a lot of, lots of, plenty of, no.*

### 9. Articles / Les articles
- *a/an* (indefinite, singular countable), *the* (definite), Ø (zero article for general plural/uncountable).
- *A dog is an animal. The dog (specific) is barking. Dogs are loyal (in general).*

### 10. Logical connectors / Les connecteurs logiques
- **Addition:** moreover, furthermore, in addition, besides.
- **Contrast:** however, nevertheless, although, though, whereas, on the other hand.
- **Cause:** because, since, as, due to, owing to.
- **Consequence:** therefore, so, thus, as a result, consequently.
- **Purpose:** so that, in order to, so as to.
- **Conclusion:** finally, in conclusion, to sum up, all in all.

### 11. Word formation / Préfixes et suffixes
- **Prefixes:** *un-* (unhappy), *in-/im-/il-/ir-* (impossible, illegal), *dis-* (disagree), *re-* (rebuild), *over-* (overpopulation), *mis-* (misunderstand).
- **Suffixes:** nouns *-tion/-sion* (education), *-ment* (development), *-ness* (happiness), *-ity* (reality); adjectives *-ful* (useful), *-less* (useless), *-able/-ible* (comfortable), *-ous* (famous); adverbs *-ly* (quickly); verbs *-ise/-ize* (modernise).
- *FR :* reconnaître préfixes/suffixes aide à **deviner le sens** d'un mot inconnu.

### 12. Common irregular verbs (base – past – past participle)
be–was/were–been · become–became–become · begin–began–begun · break–broke–broken · bring–brought–brought · buy–bought–bought · come–came–come · do–did–done · drink–drank–drunk · eat–ate–eaten · find–found–found · get–got–got(ten) · give–gave–given · go–went–gone · know–knew–known · leave–left–left · make–made–made · meet–met–met · pay–paid–paid · read–read–read · say–said–said · see–saw–seen · speak–spoke–spoken · take–took–taken · teach–taught–taught · think–thought–thought · win–won–won · write–wrote–written.

## 10 WRITING TOPICS WITH MODEL ANSWERS / 10 SUJETS D'EXPRESSION ÉCRITE

*Model answers are short and simple on purpose, to serve as realistic targets.*

**1. (Essay) "A healthy diet is more important than medicine." Discuss.**
> Both diet and medicine matter, but prevention comes first. A healthy, balanced diet gives the body the strength to resist disease, so that medicine is needed less often. Eating fruit, vegetables and enough water protects us every day, while medicine only acts once we are already ill. However, medicine remains essential when prevention fails. In my opinion, a good diet and medicine are partners: the first keeps us healthy, the second cures us when we fall ill. Still, it is wiser and cheaper to eat well than to treat avoidable diseases.

**2. (Essay) "Education is the best tool for development." Do you agree?**
> I fully agree. Education trains the doctors, engineers and teachers a developing country needs. Educated citizens find better jobs, understand their rights and make wise decisions. Education also reduces poverty and ignorance. Of course, roads, hospitals and investment are also necessary, but none of them lasts without educated people to build and manage them. Therefore, investing in schools is the surest way to build a strong nation.

**3. (Letter, informal) Write to a friend about a concert or match you attended.**
> Dear Kevin, I must tell you about the amazing match I watched last Sunday! Our town team played the champions, and the stadium was full. We shouted for ninety minutes. At the last minute, our striker scored and everyone jumped for joy. I wish you had been there — it was unforgettable. Write back soon and tell me your news. Your friend, Paul.

**4. (Report) Suggest ways to reduce traffic in your town.**
> Report: Reducing Traffic. Our town suffers from daily traffic jams. First, the council should provide more public buses, so fewer people drive alone. Secondly, old vehicles that pollute heavily should be controlled. Thirdly, roads near schools and markets should be widened. If these steps are taken, journeys will be faster and the air cleaner.

**5. (Essay) "Young people should take part in politics." Discuss.**
> Young people should certainly take part in politics, because they are the future of the nation. By voting and expressing their ideas, they can push for better schools and jobs. Some say the young lack experience, but experience is gained by taking part. A country that listens to its youth prepares its future. Therefore, I believe young people must be active, responsible citizens.

**6. (Essay) "Sport is a good way out of poverty." True or false?**
> Sport can be a way out of poverty, but not for everyone. A few talented athletes become rich and famous, and they inspire whole communities. However, for each champion, thousands train hard without success. Sport should be encouraged for health and discipline, but young people also need education as a safer path. So sport is one possible door out of poverty, not the only one.

**7. (Summary) Summarise in about 40 words a text arguing that clean water prevents disease.**
> Clean water is a simple but powerful medicine. Many diseases come from dirty water. Where families drink safe water, children are healthier and attend school more often. Bringing clean water to a village improves both health and development, often more than costly machines.

**8. (Essay) "The internet does more good than harm." Discuss.**
> The internet brings great benefits: it gives access to knowledge, connects families and creates jobs. Students can learn online, and businesses can reach new customers. However, it also spreads false information and can waste time. In my opinion, the internet does more good than harm, provided we use it wisely and critically.

**9. (Letter, formal) Apply for a scholarship.**
> Dear Sir or Madam, I am writing to apply for the scholarship offered by your foundation. I am a serious Terminale student with good results, especially in science. My family has limited means, and this scholarship would allow me to continue my studies at university. I am hard-working and determined to serve my country. I would be grateful for your kind consideration. Yours faithfully, [Name].

**10. (Essay) "Protecting the environment is everyone's duty." Discuss.**
> Protecting the environment is indeed everyone's duty, not only the government's. Each of us can avoid plastic, save water and plant trees. Pollution and deforestation threaten our health and our future. If every citizen acts, small efforts add up to a big change. In conclusion, a clean environment depends on the daily choices of ordinary people.

### BAC Paper 1 — Theme: Food and Health

## COMPLETE METHOD FOR THE ENGLISH BAC / MÉTHODE COMPLÈTE POUR RÉUSSIR LE BAC D'ANGLAIS

### 1. Know the paper (Reading 10 · Language 5 · Writing 5)
- **Reading (10 pts):** a text + comprehension questions. The biggest part — read carefully.
- **Language (5 pts):** grammar and vocabulary items (tenses, passive, word formation, connectors).
- **Writing (5 pts):** one production (letter, summary, report or argumentative essay).

### 2. Managing your time (for a typical paper)
- Spend **about half** your time on Reading (it carries half the marks).
- Then Language (quick, mechanical — secure these points).
- Keep **enough time** for Writing and for a final re-reading.
- *FR :* gardez toujours 5 minutes pour **relire** (fautes de grammaire, orthographe, ponctuation).

### 3. Reading — method
1. Read the **title** and the **whole text** once for the general idea (*skimming*).
2. Read the **questions**.
3. Read again, **underlining** the parts that answer each question (*scanning*).
4. Answer in **full, correct sentences**, reusing words from the text but **not copying blindly**.
5. For "find a word meaning…", look for a synonym in the text.
- *FR :* ne jamais laisser une question sans réponse ; même une réponse partielle rapporte des points.

### 4. Language — method
- Identify what each item tests (tense? passive? connector? prefix?).
- Apply the **rule** precisely (see the Grammar section).
- These points are the **easiest to secure**: revise the rules until they are automatic.

### 5. Writing — method
1. **Read the task** carefully (type of text? topic? number of words?).
2. **Brainstorm** ideas quickly, then **plan**: introduction → 2–3 paragraphs → conclusion.
3. Respect the **format**:
   - *Formal letter:* Dear Sir/Madam … Yours faithfully.
   - *Informal letter:* Dear [name] … Best wishes / Your friend.
   - *Report:* title + clear sections + recommendations.
   - *Essay:* introduction (state the question) → arguments with examples → conclusion (your opinion).
4. Use **connectors** (first, moreover, however, therefore, finally).
5. **Vary** vocabulary (use the thematic lists) and keep sentences **simple and correct**.
6. **Re-read**: check tenses, subject-verb agreement, spelling, punctuation.

### 6. All-year preparation
- **Read** a little English every day (the *Terminale English Reader*, simple articles).
- Keep a **vocabulary notebook** organised by the 9 themes.
- Revise **one grammar point** per week and do exercises.
- Do **one full past paper** each week under timed conditions, then correct it with the keys.
- Practise **writing** one letter/essay a week; ask your teacher to correct it.

### 7. On the exam day — golden rules
- Read **every instruction** before writing.
- **Answer everything** (never leave a blank).
- Write **clearly and legibly**.
- Keep your paragraphs **short and organised**.
- **Re-read** at the end.
- *FR :* la régularité et l'entraînement font la différence : *practice makes perfect*.

---

> **Final advice / Conseil final :** English is learned by **using** it. Read a little every day, speak without fear of mistakes, write one short text a week, and revise your thematic vocabulary. Combined with the grammar and the BAC method in this course, regular practice will carry you to success. *Good luck! / Bonne chance !*
